# Creative Research

Find creative positioning and cross-domain insights for strategy, marketing, or product. Digs for successful examples OUTSIDE the obvious domain, uncovers hidden constraints that spark ideas, and reveals positioning opportunities through divergence rather than conformity.

## When to Use

- **Positioning breakthrough** — "We're tired of being 'the mobile-first [category]'. What would we be if we owned something different?"
- **Constraint as creative** — "What if we intentionally removed X feature? What would users love instead?"
- **Cross-domain patterns** — "Who solved a similar problem in a completely different domain?"
- **Reframing angles** — "How would a minimalist/luxury/underground brand approach this market?"

Use this AFTER deep-research (you need facts first) but BEFORE brainstorming (this feeds creative fuel).

## Philosophy

**Constraints breed creativity.** Rick Rubin removes distractions to reveal what's essential. Here, we remove domain assumptions to reveal positioning opportunities.

**Divergence precedes convergence.** Successful creatives steal from everywhere. We search horizontally (other domains) before vertically (same category).

**Positioning is about ownership.** You can't own "better". You own specific, defensible territory. This skill finds adjacent territories others haven't claimed.

## The Process

### 1. Articulate Core Constraint

What's the binding constraint in your space?

Examples:
- Fitness app: "Users quit because habit formation is hard, not because the app is bad"
- B2B SaaS: "Sales cycles are long; this limits who can use the product"
- Fast fashion: "Sustainability is cost-prohibitive at scale"
- Creator tools: "Most users are non-technical; complexity is the ceiling"

Don't search for solutions yet. Get clear on the real constraint (often hidden).

### 2. Find Precedents (Not Competitors)

Search OUTSIDE your domain for brands/products that:
- **Owned the constraint**: Made it a feature, not a bug
- **Flipped the narrative**: Reframed the limitation into positioning
- **Served a different segment**: Because of the constraint, not despite it

Examples of flipped constraints:
- **Constraint**: "High latency in delivery"  
  **Flipped by**: Newspapers → "daily ritual" (timing is feature). Luxury goods → "anticipation" (wait is part of prestige).
  
- **Constraint**: "No customization possible"  
  **Flipped by**: IKEA → "users assemble it" (ownership feeling). Henry Ford → "any color as long as it's black" (honesty + scale).

- **Constraint**: "Incomplete information"  
  **Flipped by**: Netflix's algorithm → "discovery" (incompleteness is intrigue). Recommendation feeds → "serendipity".

Search task structure:

```
Find 5-7 brands/products that succeeded by OWNING a constraint similar to:
[your constraint]

For each example, identify:
- What constraint did they face?
- How did they reframe it in marketing/positioning?
- What audience did this attract?
- What would be the equivalent in [your domain]?

Look in domains like:
- Luxury goods (constraint = cost/rarity)
- Non-profit / NGOs (constraint = resources)
- Underground/indie (constraint = distribution)
- Minimalism/simplicity movements (constraint = features)
- Regional/local brands (constraint = scale)
- Creator communities (constraint = tools/gatekeeping)
```

### 3. Extract Positioning Principles

From the precedents, derive principles:

- **Principle 1**: "Constraints breed rituals. The longer the wait, the more meaningful the moment."
- **Principle 2**: "Ownership beats convenience. Let users co-create, they'll defend it."
- **Principle 3**: "Scarcity isn't a limit; it's an exclusive signal."

Map each principle back to your domain:
- **In your space**: "If we made onboarding hard but rewarding, would users feel more ownership?"
- **New positioning angle**: "For creatives who want mastery, not shortcuts."

### 4. Identify Hidden Opportunities

Use precedents to find 3 positioning gaps competitors haven't claimed:

**Gap 1** — A value your users actually care about that competitors talk down
**Gap 2** — An underserved segment that would thrive under your constraint
**Gap 3** — A narrative reframe that flips your weakness into strength

Example:
- **Gap 1**: "Slow is trustworthy" (while competitors race on features)
- **Gap 2**: "Professional craftspeople, not hobbyists" (segment that values depth over breadth)
- **Gap 3**: "Mastery through limitation" (positioning reframe: constraints aren't bugs, they're training wheels)

### 5. Divergence Pass (Red Team)

Play devil's advocate. Ask:
- "What if we're wrong about the constraint?" (Is it really binding, or is it just current orthodoxy?)
- "Who benefits from the current narrative?" (Whose incentives keep people believing the constraint is real?)
- "What would a 10x better solution require?" (Not incrementally better, but fundamentally different. Probably requires different constraints.)

Document both your divergent ideas AND the constraints they'd hit. This feeds brainstorming.

## Checklist

- [ ] **Articulate constraint** — Bind the real limitation (not the surface problem)
- [ ] **Find precedents** — 5-7 examples from other domains that flipped the constraint
- [ ] **Extract principles** — 3-5 principles from precedents; map back to your domain
- [ ] **Identify gaps** — 3 positioning opportunities competitors haven't claimed
- [ ] **Divergence pass** — Red-team your findings; document tensions and open questions

## Token Economy

- **Workers**: Use moderate models (Qwen 3.5 2b–4b) for cross-domain search + principle extraction
- **Synthesis**: Use strong model (Qwen 3.5 4b–9b) for divergence pass and positioning gap articulation
- **Hard caps**:
  - Max 7 precedent searches (beyond this is noise)
  - Max 5 principle extractions per research (avoid analysis fatigue)
  - 1 divergence pass per research (not iterative)

## Anti-Patterns

❌ **Looking only within category** — "What do competitors do?" (Narrows to existing playbook)
✅ **Cross-domain precedents** — "How do luxury brands, nonprofits, indie creators handle this?"

❌ **Listing features, not positioning** — "This brand has X feature; we could add X too"
✅ **Ownership narrative** — "This brand OWNS this audience because they committed to this constraint"

❌ **Too many principles** — Extract 10 principles → paralysis
✅ **3-5 core principles** — Actionable, memorable, defensible

❌ **No divergence** — Accept findings as final
✅ **Red-team pass** — Question constraints, challenge assumptions, document tensions
