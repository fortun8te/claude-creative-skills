# Example Deep Research Reports

## Example 1: SaaS Market Positioning (Productivity Tools)

**Topic**: Why are vertical SaaS tools (focused on one industry) outpacing horizontal tools (for multiple industries)?

**Subquestions**:
- Market size + growth rates (horizontal vs. vertical)
- Customer acquisition costs (CAC) by segment
- Why is vertical winning?

**Research findings**:

### Key Findings (Well-Established)
- Vertical SaaS is 2-3x faster growing than horizontal (Gartner 2024: 35% YoY vs. 12% YoY)
- Vertical SaaS has 40-50% lower CAC (focused on one industry = targeted marketing)
- Vertical tools have 3-5x higher NPS (industry-specific workflows, compliance built-in)

**Evidence**: Slack (horizontal, slow adoption in early days) vs. Notion (horizontal, struggled with product clarity) vs. Vetted (vertical for veterinary practices, 2-year profitable)

### Contested
- "Vertical SaaS captures more revenue per user than horizontal"
  - One side: Vertical tools lock in users deeper (compliance, integrations), justify premium pricing
  - Other side: Horizontal tools benefit from volume; total revenue higher for leaders (Slack, Notion, Figma)
  - Verdict: Contested. Vertical wins on unit economics; horizontal wins on total revenue

### Positioning Gap
- Incumbents assume "bigger TAM = better". Reality: Vertical players own compliance + integrations that matter to their customer, making them feel "made for them". Horizontal tools are generic, commoditized.

## Example 2: Vertical AI Adoption Drivers

**Topic**: Why are enterprises adopting AI agents for specific workflows, not general-purpose tools?

**Subquestions**:
- What workflows see highest AI adoption?
- Why not general-purpose AI assistants?
- What prevents wider adoption?

**Research findings**:

### Key Findings (Well-Established)
- High-ROI workflows dominate AI adoption: customer support (30% of enterprises), content generation (25%), data analysis (20%)
- Enterprises hesitate on general-purpose AI (58% report they don't trust it for mission-critical work)
- Workflow-specific AI has 5x higher adoption rate than general-purpose (Forrester 2024)

### Contested
- "Fine-tuning AI on proprietary data is necessary for enterprise adoption"
  - One side: Fine-tuning improves accuracy, compliance (keep data in-house)
  - Other side: General LLMs work fine for most workflows; fine-tuning costs exceed ROI for many enterprises
  - Verdict: Contested. Depends on workflow sensitivity + data risk

### Positioning Gap
- Vendors position AI as "replacement for humans". Reality: Enterprises see AI as "delegator for high-volume, low-judgment tasks" (support tickets, email summarization, data labeling). Safety + interpretability matter more than capability.

## Example 3: Creator Tool Positioning (Design + Video)

**Topic**: Why do creator tools (Figma, CapCut, Runway) succeed while traditional tools (Adobe, Final Cut Pro) struggle with creators?

**Subquestions**:
- Who are creators? (Size, growth, composition)
- What do they need that incumbents don't provide?
- Why the generational shift?

**Research findings**:

### Key Findings (Well-Established)
- Creator economy is 200M people globally (growing 20% YoY); 60% are Gen Z / millennials
- Creators prioritize speed of output over feature depth (Substack creator survey: 78% would trade features for 2x faster workflow)
- Cost barrier is real: Adobe subscription ($55/mo) vs. CapCut (free) is a 10x difference in onboarding friction

### Contested
- "Creators want free software because of budget"
  - One side: Cost is barrier; free removes friction
  - Other side: Creators use high-end tools when they can afford them (pros use Adobe)
  - Verdict: Contested. Budget matters for discovery; value matters for retention

### Positioning Gap
- Incumbents optimize for professionals (full feature set, deep tutorials). Creators optimize for speed (ship fast, learn as you go). Tools like Figma and CapCut own "ship fast"; Adobe owns "full featured". These are different customer segments, not features.
