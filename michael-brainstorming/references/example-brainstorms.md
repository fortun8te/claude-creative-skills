# Example Brainstorms: Approaches over Lists

## Example 1: SaaS Positioning (Productivity Tool for Teams)

**Research anchoring**:
- Deep-research findings: Vertical SaaS growing 3x faster than horizontal; NPS 5x higher
- Creative-research findings: Positioning gap = "made for them" (compliance + integrations) vs. generic

**Real Decision**: Should we target vertical (e.g., "only for agencies") or horizontal (e.g., "any team")?

---

### Approach A: Vertical-First (Target Agencies)

**Worldview**: "Ownership of a segment beats dominance of a market"
- Idea: Build exclusively for agencies. Integrations with agency tools (Asana, Airtable, QuickBooks). Compliance for agency workflows (client billing, project scoping).
- Trade-off: Smaller TAM, but higher NPS + premium pricing
- Risk: If agency segment declines, you're stuck. Harder to expand to other verticals later (product is too specific).

**Marketing angle**: "Built for agencies. By agency owners."

---

### Approach B: Horizontal-First (Start Broad)

**Worldview**: "Dominance of a market beats ownership of a segment"
- Idea: Start broad ("any team"). Add integrations by demand. Let market tell you who uses it. Expand vertically later.
- Trade-off: Slower positioning clarity, harder to win segment loyalty
- Risk: Hard to differentiate if multiple competitors chase broad market

**Marketing angle**: "The team tool that adapts to your workflow, not the other way around."

---

### Approach C: Hybrid (Vertical Entry, Horizontal Expansion)

**Worldview**: "Build credibility in a vertical, then expand via network effects"
- Idea: Launch with deep integrations for agencies (6 months). Build reputation. Then expand to other verticals (consultancies, nonprofits) with similar positioning ("professional services teams").
- Trade-off: Slower overall (two launches), but each vertical build on your credibility
- Risk: Takes 12+ months to fully expand; competitors may own broader market while you're still vertical-focused

**Marketing angle**: "We started with agencies. Now we're the default for professional services."

---

## Adversarial Thinking (Checkpoints)

**Checkpoint 1: Reframe**
- "What if 'vertical' isn't the constraint? What if it's feature parity? (i.e., agencies use generic tools because specialized tools lack features, not because they prefer generic)"
  - Implication: Approach A (vertical) fails if we can't out-feature the generalists. Approach C (hybrid) buys time to build features.

**Checkpoint 2: Premortem**
- "If we choose Approach A (vertical), what kills us in 18 months?"
  - Answer: Vertical market shrinks (agencies consolidate, or move to no-code). We have no pivot path.
  - Mitigation: Choose Approach C (vertical entry with expansion plan built-in).

**Checkpoint 3: Red Team**
- "How would Asana (or Notion) steal our vertical-first positioning?"
  - Answer: Add "agency template" and 2-3 integrations. Out-feature us with their scale.
  - Implication: Approach A only wins if we move faster than incumbents can react. Approach C buys time to build moat (reputation, network effects).

---

## Recommendation

**Choose Approach C** (Vertical Entry → Horizontal Expansion)

- Start with agencies: Deep integrations, compliance, positioning clarity
- 6-month launch + reputation build
- Then expand to other professional services (consultancies, law firms, nonprofits)
- By Month 12: "Built for professional services" (not generic "teams")

**Why**: Combines speed to credibility (Approach A) with expansion optionality (Approach B). Red-team shows incumbents can't out-vertical-feature us; they can only out-scale. Approach C mitigates by expanding early.

## Example 2: Creator Tool Campaign Strategy

**Research anchoring**:
- Deep-research findings: Creator economy is 200M people; Gen Z prioritizes speed over features
- Creative-research findings: Positioning gap = "ship fast" not "full featured"

**Real Decision**: Should we position as "speed" or "capability"?

---

### Approach A: Speed-First ("Ship 10x Faster")

**Worldview**: "Speed to output beats feature depth for creators"
- Idea: Position on workflow speed (templates, 1-click exports, drag-and-drop). "Create social content in 5 minutes." Launch with influencer creators (who ship daily) as first users.
- Trade-off: May sacrifice some customization for speed
- Risk: As creators grow more skilled, they'll outgrow the tool (limited depth)

**Campaign angle**: "5 minutes from blank canvas to published."

---

### Approach B: Capability-First ("Professional Results")

**Worldview**: "Quality of output determines creator success"
- Idea: Position on professional-grade features (color grading, typography, advanced effects). "Your portfolio looks like you hired a designer." Market to serious/aspiring creators.
- Trade-off: Higher learning curve; slower to first publish
- Risk: Loses speed-focused creators (CapCut, Canva outpace you)

**Campaign angle**: "Professional-grade design, no training required."

---

### Approach C: Speed + Capability (Tiered Learning)

**Worldview**: "Creators want speed first, capabilities as they grow"
- Idea: Launch with speed-first templates (ship in 5 min). As creators use tool, expose capabilities progressively (advanced features unlock as they skill up). "You start fast, you grow deep."
- Trade-off: Requires UI sophistication (hiding complexity, progressive disclosure)
- Risk: Execution is hard; can be confusing if not done well

**Campaign angle**: "Start in 5 minutes. Master over time."

---

## Adversarial Thinking (Checkpoints)

**Checkpoint 1: Reframe**
- "What if speed isn't what creators want? What if they want social proof (see other creators' work)?"
  - Implication: All approaches miss the real need (community). Add community features to whichever approach wins.

**Checkpoint 2: Premortem**
- "If we choose Approach A (speed), what kills us in 12 months?"
  - Answer: Creators outgrow the tool; they need more power but feel locked in. They migrate to Adobe/Figma.
  - Mitigation: Approach C (speed → capabilities) allows growth path.

**Checkpoint 3: Red Team**
- "How would TikTok or Instagram steal our speed positioning?"
  - Answer: Add creator tools natively. Out-distribute us via platform.
  - Implication: Speed alone isn't defensible. Add community (social proof) + capabilities (growth path) to Approach C.

---

## Recommendation

**Choose Approach C** (Speed + Capability Tiering) **+ Community Feature**

- Launch campaign: "Create in 5 minutes"
- Feature story: "Creators share templates + get social proof" (community is the stickiness)
- Growth messaging: "Master advanced features as you scale"

**Why**: Speed gets creators in the door. Community keeps them. Capabilities prevent churn to Pro tools. Red-team shows pure speed isn't defensible; Approach C + community addresses all three vectors.
